# Cleaning plan: first-week teaching

Research checked October 16, 2026. Scope: the home plan's first seven lessons;
keep its scheduled activities and later lessons intact.

These are public course descriptions and free teaching resources. Paid course
materials were not accessed. The lessons use original prose, scenarios, and
interactions; the seven-day sequence combines these teaching patterns rather
than reproducing any provider's course.

## First-week progression

1. Choose what the space needs to let you do; distinguish decluttering, tidying,
   and cleaning.
2. Use a small category to practise keep decisions, with Marie Kondo's approach
   explained and attributed.
3. Give frequently used belongings an easy return place near their use.
4. Clear first, clean surfaces from high to low, then finish with floors.
5. Decide what leaves before buying storage; give donations an exit plan.
6. Choose a small suitable cleaning kit and use products and cloths correctly.
7. Separate daily upkeep, weekly tasks, and occasional jobs; choose a minimum
   version that makes returning possible.

Each lesson should teach an independent household skill, include a realistic
decision or ordering exercise, and finish with one bounded action. Existing
scheduled breathing, grounding, and muscle-release practices remain brief
supporting asides. Historical lesson IDs stay available for saved reads.

## Beginner reading level

Assume the reader has never learned a cleaning routine. Explain a new word
before asking them to use it. For example, a category means one type of thing,
such as socks; an item's home means the place where it is kept.

Use short sentences and everyday words. Show what the reader actually picks
up, where they put it, and when the job is finished. Add a worked example when
an instruction could otherwise mean several things. Explain why the order
matters. Keep the tone respectful and adult.

Longer teaching is welcome when it answers a real question. Keep paragraphs
focused so the existing lesson reader can show them as short slides. Simplify
quiz questions, answer feedback, and the final action as well as the main text.
Keep product safety instructions precise when replacing technical words.
