# Stress and overthinking: full-course comparison

Reviewed October 16, 2026. This extends the earlier first-week research to both
complete plans. The final scope is 28 days (four weeks) each, following the
user's duration change. The new edition retains the first 28 days of the existing
activity schedule. Earlier enrollments keep their saved curriculum and length;
the anger track remains on its existing 56-day edition.

These are public descriptions, not access to the complete paid courses or their
private teaching materials. We adopt teaching methods and broad progression,

## Where our earlier edition fell short

The audit resolved all 112 daily lessons across both tracks in pressure edition
12, including goal variants, first-week replacements, and gentle-week swaps.
It found:

- The opening week had clear, practical teaching. Later weeks often changed a
  shared lesson's opening and final action while keeping examples about another
  task in its middle. A worry lesson could switch from checking a message to
  drafting a work report without explaining the connection.
- Many later questions repeated the opening instead of asking the reader to
  use a skill in a new situation.
- Whole weeks concentrated on small variations of pauses or rest. Thought
  patterns and dealing with uncertainty received less progressive practice.
- Weekly copy described a broad theme, but daily lessons did not consistently
  build toward a weekly skill or review a real attempt.

The replacement teaches each day in the track's own language. It uses one main
skill, a worked example, a decision or ordering activity, and a concrete action.
The last day of each module reviews what the reader tried. Progress means a
useful action or clearer decision, not an empty mind or guaranteed calm.

## New four-week curriculum

| Week | Stress | Overthinking | End-of-week evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Recognize stress and try a small toolkit | Recognize repeated thinking and try a small toolkit | Choose one response to use again |
| 2 | Notice extra effort, take useful breaks, and use fairer thoughts | Practise attention returns and compare useful preparation with repeated worry | Describe an action that fitted the situation |
| 3 | Choose priorities, set limits, and ask for specific support | Solve an actual problem and separate facts from guesses | Try an action and review what happened |
| 4 | Protect recovery, handle setbacks, and plan ongoing support | Handle ordinary uncertainty, evenings, and returning worries | Write a personal continuation plan |

Days 1–7 retain the recently rewritten starter lessons. Days 8–28 receive new,
distinct lesson IDs. The first week introduces options; later modules explain
and practise them rather than assuming they were mastered in a day.

## Deliberate adaptations

- Preserve the app's short daily format instead of equating a daily lesson with
  a two-hour class or a clinical treatment module.
- Keep the existing breathing, grounding, and muscle-release schedule. Each
  scheduled practice is a brief supporting aside; the household or thinking
  skill is taught independently.
- Use plain adult language, explain unfamiliar terms, and show what to do next
  and when to stop. Do not hide the instructions behind course terminology.
- Do not reproduce panic treatment, medication guidance, exposure protocols,
  diagnostic questionnaires, or insomnia treatment in a general wellbeing plan.
- Distinguish ordinary nonurgent uncertainty from situations requiring timely
  health, safety, financial, or practical action. Do not turn notes or checking
  into rituals that must be repeated until the reader feels certain.
- Preserve older pressure editions and the anger track. Weekly descriptions
  should follow the enrollment's edition as well as its saved track.

## Validation

Verify all 28 days resolve to distinct lessons in each new track, new and older
