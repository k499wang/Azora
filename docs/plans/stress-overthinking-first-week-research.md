# Stress and overthinking: first-week teaching

Research checked October 16, 2026. Scope: the first seven lessons of the stress
and overthinking pressure tracks. Keep the existing activity schedule, anger
track, later lessons, and pre-trial lesson definitions intact.

## Public sources

- [NHS Fife: Step on Stress](https://www.accesstherapiesfife.scot.nhs.uk/step-on-stress/)
  describes a three-session course: understand personal stress patterns, learn
  about body responses, then work with thoughts. This supports beginning with
  recognition and building practical skills in order.
- [WHO: Doing What Matters in Times of Stress](https://www.who.int/thailand/activities/doing-what-matters-in-times-of-stress)
  describes skills for noticing the present, stepping back from thoughts,
  meaningful action, accepting feelings, and kindness. Use the broad teaching
  progression, with original everyday examples and wording.
- [NHS: stress](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-health-issues/stress/)
  and [stress management tips](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/tips-to-reduce-stress/)
  support manageable tasks, social support, breaks, and attention to what can
  be changed. Stress teaching should address demands as well as responses.
- [CCI: Worry and Rumination](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Worry-and-Rumination)
  presents a sequential workbook progressing through understanding worry,
  attention, problem solving, helpful thinking, uncertainty, and self-management.
  Use a short introductory progression rather than presenting the app as the
  complete treatment course.
- [NHS: tackling worries](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/tackling-your-worries/)
  distinguishes actionable problems from hypothetical worries and describes a
  short planned worry period. Teach what to do at that time and how to finish;
  postponement does not apply to urgent action.
- [NHS: reframing thoughts](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/reframing-unhelpful-thoughts/)
  supports checking evidence and considering a balanced view. Do not require
  forced positive thinking or certainty.
- [NHS: problem solving](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/problem-solving/)
  supports a specific plan: what to do, what help is needed, and when to do it.

These sources inform the lesson structure and skills. The app's lessons use
original prose, examples, questions, and actions. They do not reproduce course
worksheets or imply endorsement by the source organizations.

## Additional course comparison

- [Stress Control, published by Stepiau](https://www.stepiau.org/en/stress-control)
  builds from understanding stress to body responses, thoughts, actions, and
  finally bringing the skills together. Azora's opening week ends by revisiting
  its original dinner-and-messages example and applying the skills to it.
- [THIS WAY UP: Worry and Rumination](https://thiswayup.org.au/what-we-treat/worry-and-rumination)
  uses story-based modules, practical action plans, and time to practise between
  modules. Azora adopts the story-to-practice pattern using its own unanswered
  message example, returns to it in day seven, and explains how repeated thinking
  can concern a past conversation as well as a future event.
- [SilverCloud stress programme overview](https://silvercloud.zendesk.com/hc/article_attachments/11021702454813)
  publicly describes understanding stress, coping, taking action, and working
  with thoughts, supported by interactive activities and personal stories. The
  indexed public outline supports this comparison; its attachment could not be
  fetched directly, so no detailed curriculum or paid material was used.

These comparisons support the first-week structure, not a claim that seven
daily lessons reproduce a complete treatment programme. Keep time for real-life
practice; completing a lesson is not evidence that a skill has been mastered.

## First-week progression

| Day | Stress | Overthinking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Notice a personal stress pattern | Recognize thinking that repeats without new information |
| 2 | Pause before accepting another request | Separate an actionable problem from a what-if question |
| 3 | Notice the current situation and choose one task | Return to an ordinary activity while a thought remains |
| 4 | Use actual deadlines to choose a visible next step | Make a concrete plan for one solvable problem |
| 5 | Reduce, share, or renegotiate a demand | Separate facts from guesses and write a balanced statement |
| 6 | Take a brief comfortable recovery break | Try a short planned worry review with a clear ending |
| 7 | Keep a useful response and plan support | Make a small return plan for the next repeated question |

## Reading and behavior constraints

Assume no prior knowledge. Explain each skill using a familiar situation, then
show the exact action and stopping point. Prefer short sentences and everyday
words. Explain a term when it first appears. Longer teaching should clarify a
real question and remain readable as the app's short slides. Questions, answer
feedback, and closing tasks need the same care as the main text.

Keep scheduled breathing, grounding, and muscle-release activities as brief
supporting asides on their existing days. The lesson should teach a useful
everyday skill independently of the activity. Avoid new breathing instructions,
diagnoses, claims that every symptom is stress, or promises to remove thoughts
and feelings. Do not make repeated checking or rewriting notes a requirement.
Review once, choose an available action, and leave room for unanswered questions.

Running pressure plans identify their track from the saved first lesson ID.
When introducing a new opening lesson, keep both the earlier and current IDs
recognized by `pressureLessonTrackForEnrollment`. Saved plans should continue
to show guidance for the track they started with.
