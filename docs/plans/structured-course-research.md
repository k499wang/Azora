# Structured course research and proposed Azora curriculum

Researched October 17, 2026. Proposal for editorial review; no shipped lessons or enrollments changed.

## Research findings

We examined public program descriptions and module outlines, not complete paid curricula. The sequences below are original Azora proposals. Source programs' treatment evidence and endorsements do not transfer to Azora.

| Source | Published structure | Teaching pattern to adopt |
| --- | --- | --- |

Use broad concepts and teaching methods with original wording, stories, questions, and activities. Do not reproduce worksheets, illustrations, scripts, distinctive exercises, or substantial course text without appropriate permission. Links document provenance, not endorsement.

## What changes from current Azora

The current stress and overthinking courses already have a four-week arc, examples, interactions, actions, and reviews. Preserve useful material. Audit each lesson against an explicit module outcome rather than rewriting for tone alone.

The current pressure lesson builder fixes each lesson to three teaching paragraphs, an interaction, a choice, and an action. That is a presentation format, not a learning progression. Author the learning objective first; let a practice or review day reuse a skill without inventing a new concept to fill the format.

The existing previous-action follow-up is a useful foundation. Its response currently drives local feedback. Persisting and using responses to choose future exercises is a separate implementation task.

## Course design standard

Every course needs:

1. An observable course outcome, stated without promising symptom reduction.
2. A module map explaining which skill each module develops and what it builds on.
3. Lesson objectives expressed as something the learner can do.
4. Worked examples followed by a different situation for guided application.
5. Real-world practice and a later review of the attempt.
6. A final application combining skills, plus an easier fallback and a continuation plan.

Lesson pattern: objective → explanation → worked example → guided application with explanatory feedback → personal action with a clear endpoint → takeaway.

Practice days and review days have their own purpose. Do not force every day into new teaching. A checked lesson records participation; it does not prove mastery. Self-reported improvement is separate from demonstrated skill and activity completion.

## Proposed four-week overthinking curriculum

Outcome: identify an everyday repetitive-thinking pattern, distinguish an actionable concern from a hypothetical one, choose a suitable response, and return to an activity. This is a wellbeing curriculum, not a diagnostic or treatment protocol.

Each row contains seven daily units. Days 3 and 5 emphasize practice; day 7 reviews an attempt. The 28-day format fits the current course, not an established optimal learning duration.

| Module | Daily units in order | End-of-module evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Recognize your pattern | Separate event, interpretation, response; distinguish thinking from making progress; map one ordinary example; notice what repeated checking changes; try an alternative next action; apply it in a different situation; review the attempt | Describe one pattern and identify a possible alternative response |
| 2. Choose where attention goes | Understand attention shifts; choose a concrete task target; practise returning; distinguish a return from suppressing thoughts; continue a small task with a thought present; troubleshoot distraction without restarting everything; review the attempt | Demonstrate one return and explain that success does not require thoughts to disappear |
| 3. Decide what needs action | Distinguish actionable concerns from hypothetical predictions; describe a solvable problem; choose one feasible action; separate facts from interpretations; compare preparation with repeated guessing; apply the decision process to a new example; review a personal attempt | Make an appropriate practical decision with a visible endpoint |
| 4. Use skills independently | Recognize ordinary uncertainty; identify when more information would actually change a decision; practise moving on after sufficient preparation; choose a response for an evening loop; combine skills in a new scenario; write a personal response and fallback plan; review and continue | Produce a usable plan and explain a response to a new situation |

Do not encourage postponing necessary health, safety, financial, or practical action. Use low-stakes ordinary scenarios for uncertainty practice. Do not introduce checking quotas, exposure protocols, or requirements to repeat an exercise until a feeling changes.

## Proposed four-week procrastination curriculum

Outcome: identify an avoidance pattern, define a manageable starting action, arrange a realistic opportunity to begin, and adjust after an unsuccessful attempt.

| Module | Skills in order | End-of-module evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Understand your starting barrier | Name one avoided task; identify the moment of avoidance; notice immediate relief and later consequences; distinguish uncertainty, task size, and unpleasantness; choose one barrier to address; attempt a start; review | Describe a personal avoidance pattern without making it a character judgment |
| 2. Make the task startable | Define a visible next action; gather needed materials; choose an opportunity; reduce competing demands; try a short start; adjust a step that was too large; review | Produce and try a specific starting plan |
| 3. Respond when motivation is low | Recognize waiting-for-motivation reasoning; choose a realistic alternative; notice uncomfortable feelings; start an ordinary manageable task without requiring enthusiasm; address perfectionistic scope; practise again; review | Choose a strategy that fits the barrier and report an attempt |
| 4. Keep going and restart | Identify what helped; repeat in another context; use an easier fallback; distinguish a genuine constraint from a plan that needs adjusting; troubleshoot a missed attempt; write a personal starting plan; complete a final application | Independently plan a start and explain how to adapt it |

This proposal is not a mapping to a shipped procrastination enrollment. Audit the relevant current plan before wiring lesson IDs or changing its schedule.

## Original sample lesson: Give preparation a finish line

**Objective:** Distinguish useful preparation from repeated prediction, then define an endpoint for one ordinary preparation task.

**Explanation:** Preparation changes what you know or what is ready. Repeated prediction can revisit the same possibility without producing new information. Ask what the next check would change. A finish line is a practical condition, such as confirming a meeting location and putting the address in your calendar.

**Worked example:** Sam has a routine meeting in a building they have not visited. They confirm the address, check the journey, and save the appointment. They keep reopening the map, although nothing has changed. Sam's preparation is complete when the address and travel plan are saved. They can check again if the meeting location changes.

**Guided application:** Alex is attending a class. The organizer has just emailed a different entrance. Should Alex use the new information, or treat checking as repetitive worry?

**Feedback:** Use the new information. A changed entrance affects the plan. The skill is deciding whether a check has a practical purpose, not avoiding all checking.

**Personal action:** Choose one ordinary upcoming activity. Name what information or materials you need, get those ready, and write one sentence describing when preparation is complete. Use that endpoint unless circumstances change.

**Next review:** What did you prepare? What was your endpoint? Did anything genuinely change afterward? If the endpoint was unclear, make it more concrete for the next attempt.

**Takeaway:** Prepare until the practical requirement is met. Respond to changed circumstances when they occur.

## Implementation sequence

1. Review the proposed outcomes and map existing lessons to them. Identify missing teaching, unnecessary repetition, and prerequisites introduced too late.
2. Rewrite one complete module with original material. Review psychological accuracy, accessibility, feedback quality, and boundaries with an appropriately qualified reviewer before making clinical claims.
3. Fit the approved module to existing lesson blocks. Change the renderer only for a demonstrated teaching need.
4. Add explicit module outcomes and review experiences; distinguish work that needs new persistence from content-only changes.
5. Publish through new curriculum editions. Preserve saved schedules and historical lessons for existing enrollments, following the current revision contracts.
6. Validate objective-to-exercise alignment, source accuracy, every lesson's resolution, old enrollment compatibility, and the rendered reading experience. No new course is approved merely because its data passes tests.

## Summary of learnings

The useful common pattern is a coherent progression from understanding a pattern to practising responses and applying them independently. Azora already has much of the presentation machinery. Its editorial work should make objectives, prerequisites, practice, and final outcomes explicit while retaining short lessons and original content.
