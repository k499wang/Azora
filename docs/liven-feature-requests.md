# Liven feature requests and valued features: concrete product examples

Research date: October 11, 2026. App: **Liven: AI Life Coach**, US App Store ID `6450840109`.

This follows the format of [Chillio's feature requests](chillio-feature-requests.md): what reviewers value or request, a concrete screen or flow, and identifiable evidence. **The interfaces, controls, and example dialogue below are proposals, not descriptions of verified current Liven behavior.** Dialogue is illustrative, not quoted from reviews. This is competitor research, not an approved Azora implementation plan.

## Review sample

The research covered 500 unique written reviews exposed by Apple's public US feed, updated June 6–October 9, 2026, plus the ten reviews displayed on the supplied page. Two displayed reviews match feed entries, so those samples are not added together. This is a recent accessible window, not all historical reviews; the feed cannot confirm iPhone-only use.

The [Liven review collection](liven-app-store-reviews.md) contains coverage, rating distribution, and 50 representative summaries with reviewer names, exact update timestamps, ratings, app versions, IDs, and source links. Each evidence link below opens an individual entry. Dates may reflect edits; complaints and benefits are user reports. Historical complaints do not establish that a feature remains absent today.

Unlike the Chillio report, this analysis did not count all feature mentions. Categories below are qualitative findings, not a frequency ranking.

## 1. Help me understand this feeling, rather than give general advice

**What reviewers value:** AmyL-M describes venting, feeling validated, and developing coping strategies. Mamamorty particularly appreciates follow-up questions that deepen reflection.

**What it would look like:** The user opens **Talk it through** and types:

> I felt awful after a meeting even though nothing obviously went wrong.

The companion acknowledges the experience and asks one relevant question, such as:

> Was it something someone said, or the feeling of being watched?

After the answer, it offers a tentative interpretation the user can confirm or reject. **Explore more** continues the conversation; **Find one next step** helps turn the reflection into an action.

The defining behavior is a conversation that responds to what the person actually said and helps them reach their own understanding.

**Evidence:** AmyL-M, US, 5/5, updated September 3, 2026, review ID `14507554057` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14507554057)); Mamamorty, US, 5/5, updated June 28, 2026, ID `14239405991` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14239405991)).

## 2. Remember the context that makes guidance personal

**What reviewers value or miss:** Cinderjamie says successive conversations become more personalized. Disappointed84621 reports losing warmth, remembered context, and useful response depth after an update.

**What it would look like:** Someone returns after previously discussing difficult work meetings. The companion asks:

> Last time, preparing one question helped you feel more comfortable. Would that help today?

The user can select **Yes**, **Something changed**, or **Don't use that context**. A **Remembered context** view lets them correct or remove saved information.

Remembering a fact should lead to more relevant guidance. The proposed controls let the user correct context rather than repeatedly explain themselves.

**Evidence:** Cinderjamie, US, 5/5, updated September 16, 2026, ID `14557860952` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14557860952)); Disappointed84621, US, 1/5, updated June 26, 2026, ID `14230119478` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14230119478)).

## 3. A short journal entry that gives me something back

**What reviewers value:** cindecker praises prompts that reveal deeper thoughts. ManorChick prefers brief dictated check-ins to long journaling. Frank1961! also uses microphone input.

**What it would look like:** **Check in** offers **Type** and **Dictate**, then asks:

> What stayed on your mind today?

After a short entry, the app presents a suggested feeling, the event connected to it, and one optional follow-up question. The user can edit that interpretation before saving. **Save and finish** ends the check-in immediately; **Go deeper** continues.

The defining behavior is useful reflection with a small amount of input. Dictation here means speaking an entry; the reviews do not establish a need for full spoken back-and-forth coaching.

**Evidence:** cindecker, US, 5/5, updated June 14, 2026, ID `14181740635` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14181740635)); ManorChick, US, 5/5, updated June 15, 2026, ID `14185750454` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14185750454)); Frank1961!, US, 5/5, updated June 13, 2026, ID `14177984528` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14177984528)).

## 4. Give me a familiar coping exercise when I cannot think clearly

**What reviewers value:** CatOrWall says the companion recalls techniques when emotional activation makes them difficult to remember. Melody Callis reports feeling calmer after a brief box-breathing exercise.

**What it would look like:** **Help me settle** opens a small set of familiar options. If the user previously found breathing useful, it appears as an optional suggestion. **Start breathing** opens the exercise immediately, with **Pause** and **End** available.

Afterward, **How do you feel now?** offers **Better**, **About the same**, and **Worse**, with an optional note. The user can finish or choose another tool.

The defining behavior is reducing the effort of finding something useful in the moment. These reviews describe perceived benefits; they do not establish clinical effectiveness or population-wide demand for breathing.

**Evidence:** CatOrWall, US, 5/5, updated July 9, 2026, ID `14281856621` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14281856621)); Melody Callis, US, 5/5, updated June 11, 2026, ID `14172470828` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14172470828)).

## 5. One manageable lesson with an exercise I can actually try

**What reviewers value:** Guitarman_83 appreciates short, interactive learning. laughingfishy describes daily lessons as understandable and applicable.

**What it would look like:** **Today's lesson** presents one idea, a short example, and a question tied to the user's day. An illustrative lesson about assumptions might ask:

> What did you assume the other person meant? What else could explain it?

**Try this today** saves the exercise; **Read again** revisits the explanation. Completed lessons remain accessible.

The defining behavior is that the user can understand and apply one idea without working through a large amount of reading. The sample supports manageable lessons; a particular duration or lesson format would need testing.

**Evidence:** Guitarman_83, US, 5/5, updated June 18, 2026, ID `14198900538` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14198900538)); laughingfishy, US, 5/5, updated June 10, 2026, ID `14167696213` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14167696213)).

## 6. Let me choose the next journey after finishing one

**What reviewers request:** ATD1710 enjoyed a course but exhausted its content. Another reviewer wants to generate a second plan. Bjamesalex reports needing customer support to unlock another journey without being shown the available options.

**What it would look like:** Completing a journey opens **Choose what to work on next**, with an accessible list of topics, short descriptions, and **Start this journey**. Illustrative topics might include work stress or maintaining a routine.

**Help me choose** offers a brief questionnaire. **Repeat previous journey** remains available. The user can begin another plan directly from the completion screen.

The defining behavior is that completing a course leads to a clear, self-service next step.

**Evidence:** ATD1710, US, 4/5, updated July 15, 2026, ID `14307759447` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14307759447)); 123445678901010101, US, 3/5, updated July 15, 2026, ID `14307856949` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14307856949)); Bjamesalex, US, 2/5, updated June 13, 2026, ID `14178204965` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14178204965)).

## 7. Turn a small promise into a task I can repeat

**What reviewers value or request:** AlFerre appreciates completing small tasks that encourage real-world action. CLC2727! values an achievable plan for tomorrow. Working onmyself explicitly requests daily or weekly repetition and saved custom tasks.

**What it would look like:** After a reflection, the user saves an illustrative action such as **Take a short walk after lunch**. Its task editor offers:

- **When:** today or tomorrow.
- **Repeat:** none, daily, or weekly.
- **Save as a reusable task:** keep it available for another plan.

On the day, **Done**, **Move to tomorrow**, and **Make it smaller** let the user adapt the action. Completing it records progress.

The defining behavior is that an achievable action can become a routine without being entered from scratch each time. The controls beyond repetition and reusable tasks are proposed examples.

**Evidence:** AlFerre, US, 5/5, updated June 9, 2026, ID `14164637667` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14164637667)); CLC2727!, US, 5/5, updated June 6, 2026, ID `14152043068` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14152043068)); Working onmyself, US, 4/5, updated June 15, 2026, ID `14185029382` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14185029382)).

## 8. Keep the exercise, my answer, and its summary together

**What reviewers value or request:** Amber the drywall saleswoman appreciates reopening conversations. Georgiaofthemtns wants interactive exercises, responses, and summaries available later, and currently takes screenshots.

**What it would look like:** **My reflections** contains saved conversations and completed exercises. Opening an exercise shows its original prompt, the user's answer, the saved summary, and the date.

**Read my previous answer** revisits the record. **Try again** starts a new dated response while keeping the old one, so the user can see how their thinking changed.

The defining behavior is that useful work remains accessible after its session ends, including what the user contributed.

**Evidence:** Amber the drywall saleswoman, US, 5/5, updated June 18, 2026, ID `14197548705` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14197548705)); Georgiaofthemtns, US, 4/5, updated August 26, 2026, ID `14473222949` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14473222949)).

## 9. Let me decide how I feel, then show the pattern over time

**What one reviewer values and misses:** HeadySolstice liked logging moods throughout a day and reviewing daily or weekly patterns. They report that a redesign replaced that control with AI interpretations they found inaccurate.

**What it would look like:** The user chooses their mood and optionally describes what happened. Any suggested mood is editable before saving. **My week** displays the entries in order so a difficult event does not automatically define the entire day.

Opening an entry shows the recorded mood, context, and date; **Correct this entry** lets the user fix it.

The defining behavior is that interpretation remains subordinate to the person's own account. This specific control request is supported by one detailed review, not a measured majority.

**Evidence:** HeadySolstice, US, 2/5, updated June 18, 2026, ID `14199637574` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14199637574)).

## 10. Let a check-in acknowledge physical discomfort

**What one reviewer requests:** Mom2tman asks for physical-symptom logging, links between symptoms and mood, and customizable daily questions. They find purely emotional framing mismatched to their experience of chronic physical symptoms.

**What it would look like:** An optional **Physical discomfort** section lets the user record symptoms they choose to track and indicate whether these affected their mood. A check-in could say:

> What affected your day: emotions, physical discomfort, or both?

**Customize my check-in** lets the user include relevant questions. A saved entry preserves their account without assigning a medical cause.

The defining behavior is recognizing the context the user reports. This is a specific request from one reviewer, not evidence of broad demand or a specification for medical monitoring.

**Evidence:** Mom2tman, US, 3/5, updated June 9, 2026, ID `14163615331` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14163615331)).

## Access should stay simple

Dumplindidit complains about repeated upgrade offers; Imnotsuremz describes repeated rating prompts, including after already reviewing. A concrete response would be to open purchased tools directly, make offers dismissible, and avoid recurring feedback interruptions. These are experience improvements supported by complaints, rather than features reviewers praise.

**Evidence:** Dumplindidit, US, 1/5, updated October 8, 2026, ID `14642458530` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14642458530)); Imnotsuremz, US, 1/5, updated July 9, 2026, ID `14284133079` ([individual review](liven-app-store-reviews.md#review-14284133079)).

## Product opportunity and summary of learnings

The combined flow suggested by the evidence is:

**Describe what happened → understand the feeling → choose a useful exercise or small action → save the reflection → return to it later.**

For Azora, a concrete first experiment could connect a brief check-in to an optional breathing session and a saved reflection. Guided prompts and a small next action could then extend that flow. The proposed sequence is a design interpretation; the reviews do not establish conversion, retention, or effectiveness. The important distinction for discovery is between praised experiences worth preserving and explicit gaps worth validating.
