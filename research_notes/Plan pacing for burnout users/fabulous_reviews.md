# Fabulous (thefabulous.co): why users stay or quit, with a focus on pacing

Research date: 2026-10-20. Researcher notes for a report writer.

**Sampling (read this first)**
- **Sample A: Trustpilot, 63 reviews.** These are pages 1–3 of trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co, sorted most recent first, dated 18 Aug to 19 Oct 2026. Some reviews have no body text. I tallied the themes by hand from the fetched text: [p1](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co), [p2](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=2), [p3](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=3).
- **Sample B: justuseapp, 39 reviews.** About 31 are App Store reviews (mostly undated, roughly 2019–2024) and 8 are dated Trustpilot-style reviews from 2021–2025. The theme counts come from a fetch-model summary that I spot-checked, so treat them as approximate: [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1203637303/fabulous-daily-routine-planner/reviews).
- **Sample C: Apple's US App Store review page, 10 distinct reviews.** These are dated 2019–2024. Apple surfaces "helpful" reviews, which skews positive: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fabulous-daily-habit-tracker/id1203637303?see-all=reviews).
- **Total: about 112 reviews.** I read them one by one in summarized form. I have no full verbatim text for most, because the fetch tool caps quotes at about 125 characters.
- **Reddit was not accessible.** reddit.com is blocked for both search and fetch in this environment, so I have **no first-hand Reddit data**. The only Reddit quotes below come second-hand through a competitor's blog (Headway).
- **Google Play could not be read.** The page returned no review content.
- **Selection bias.** Reviewers are self-selected and polarized: on Trustpilot, 52% give 5 stars and 28% give 1 star. Trustpilot is also where billing disputes go. Fabulous solicits reviews in-app, which inflates "just started, love it" 5-star reviews. Neither sample shows how many silent users churned, or why.

## Q1: What are the most common reasons users praise or abandon Fabulous?

### Takeaway
In current reviews, the main reason people leave angry is **billing, trial and cancellation practices**, not habit pacing. They outnumber every product complaint combined on Trustpilot. Among product complaints, the most common is **clutter, overwhelm and upsell noise**: too many sub-apps, too much text, a confusing UI. "Too slow / boring / only water" is a minor theme. Praise centres on **gentle, small-steps pacing**, the check-off structure and encouraging reminders.

- The "overwhelming" complaints are about **UI density, text volume and cross-promotion**, not about adding one habit a week. The slow habit cadence sits inside a noisy shell.

## Q2: Is the slow pacing a reason people stay or leave?

### Takeaway
On balance, the slow pacing is a **retention positive in reviews**. People who stay credit "baby steps" and "doesn't rush you." "Too slow / only water" complaints are rare (about 3 of 63 recent Trustpilot reviews). They come from users who **already had basic habits**, and they are tied to paying: "paying for water." The pacing failure mode that shows up more often is the opposite one. Users stack too many habits into a ritual early and burn out, sometimes against the app's own advice.

### Cited Findings
- **"Too slow / trivial" complaints**
  - Title: "That paying for regular intake of water is not worth doing." The reviewer adds, "i would prefer something vigorous." (2★, 2 Oct 2026) — [Trustpilot p2](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=2)
  - Title: "No choice of goals." Quote: "There didn't seem to be any choice over my goal. The first goal was something I already do." (1★, 4 Oct 2026) — same page
  - "many elements are counterintuitive to the new user, especially one who already has a base of good habits." (3★, 21 Sep 2026) — [Trustpilot p3](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=3)
  - One App Store reviewer found the water challenge useless and the app "a bit boring and not really motivating." That review was paired with a trial-billing complaint. I saw it only via a search snippet, not the full review. — [App Store JP/US listing via search](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id1203637303)
- **Slow pacing praised**
  - "Fabulous doesn't rush you, make you feel small or insignificant." (5★, 6 Oct 2026) — [Trustpilot p2](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=2)
  - Title: "Well-designed systems." The reviewer likes that the app introduces only a few tasks at a time and builds gradually, which makes the plan sustainable. (5★, 15 Oct 2026; paraphrased by the fetch tool) — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co)
  - "it starts with baby steps to ensure that you get a feeling of success" (App Store, 1 Feb 2022) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fabulous-daily-habit-tracker/id1203637303?see-all=reviews)
  - "I loved that it said we never want to take on more than we should … there is absolutely no rush." (App Store, 11 Oct 2024, ADHD user) — same source
  - "suggesting that we slow down and enjoy the process" ("There is something so special about Fabulous," 26 Jan 2022) — same source
  - "Not only have I finally been able to slow down enough" (App Store, 2 Nov 2022, three days in, enjoying the water "ding") — same source
- **Overload from stacking habits into rituals**
  - The "Fabulous" review on justuseapp says "Slow down" and reports "too much" habits by the end of month one. — [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1203637303/fabulous-daily-routine-planner/reviews)
  - "Hot mess express" "loaded a morning routine of way too many items." She says going big was not what the app recommended. — same source
  - "so as to not overwhelm myself when I add routines" (App Store, 1 Nov 2024, depression) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fabulous-daily-habit-tracker/id1203637303?see-all=reviews)
- **How the pacing is designed**
  - The first journey's only goal for 3 days is a glass of water on waking, with a daily notification. — [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/healthy/fabulous-app-review)
  - The app starts with morning routines and "slowly expands into evening routines." Journeys are "multi-week programs." — [Headway blog](https://makeheadway.com/blog/fabulous-app-review/)
- **Repetition over months**
  - Nibble's review says Journey content "can feel repetitive after a few months of use." Healthline notes the journeys are not very flexible or customizable. Both are secondary sources seen through a search summary. — [Nibble](https://nibble-app.com/blog/fabulous-app-review), [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/healthy/fabulous-app-review)
- **Letters**
  - "You're writing books and no one has time for that." "Feels like a cult." (App Store via justuseapp, undated) — [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1203637303/fabulous-daily-routine-planner/reviews)
  - Another review on the same page praises letters "timed to arrive right when I needed them" ("Evidence-Based Behavioral Change"). — same source
  - "Weird letter reading VO" ("Hate the illustrations") — same source
  - Long-form blog reviews say the intro letter is like "reading a book" and suggest making letters shorter. I saw this via a search summary of [RagsToNiche$](https://ragstoniches.com/fabulous-app-review-how-fabulous-is-it) and [Geekbea](https://geekbea.com/fabulous-app-review/) and did not verify it verbatim.
- **Guilt and shame**
  - The one explicit shame mention credits the app with *relieving* shame: "I became walking shame" describes the reviewer's state before the app, which then told them "to slow down and take it easy." (App Store, 1 Nov 2024) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fabulous-daily-habit-tracker/id1203637303?see-all=reviews)
  - Two Trustpilot complaints are about missed-day *mechanics*, not guilt: "I skipped some days, but the program didn't reflect it." and "it assumes you didn't perform those activities and only lets you start with the current day." (Sep 2026) — [Trustpilot p3](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=3)

### Inferences
- Slow pacing splits users by starting point. People starting from low function (depression, ADHD, burnout, "bed rotting") experience it as safety. People who already have baseline habits experience it as condescending, especially when they are paying from day one.
- The real overload risk is **ritual stacking**. Users who add many habits at once, even against the app's advice, report "too much." A good design move would be to **hard-gate** additions rather than only advising against them.
- Letters split users. They are delighted when short and well timed, and see them as preachy or "book"-length otherwise. The complaints are a minority but they recur.
- Guilt is not a visible abandonment driver in the reviews I sampled. This may be survivorship: people who felt ashamed may just leave quietly.

### Gaps
- I found no source that counts "too slow" against "too much" across a large sample.
- I found no Fabulous retention data by journey week. I could not see whether churn spikes after the water step.
- I could not verify the Reddit-specific "only drink water" sentiment.

## Q3: What do users with ADHD, burnout or depression say?

### Takeaway
ADHD and depression users are **overrepresented among the enthusiastic reviews**. They credit small wins, sounds, check marks, timers and "no rush." The ADHD criticism is specific and consistent: the **interface** is noisy, wordy and hard to navigate, which is a separate issue from habit pace. I found nothing that explicitly says "burnout."

### Cited Findings
- **Positive ADHD and depression reviews**
  - Title: "Perfect for an ADHD brain that forgets its own progress." Quote: "What I appreciate most is the focus on small wins, reminders, and little moments of progress throughout the day." (5★, 22 Sep 2026) — [Trustpilot p3](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=3)
  - "As someone one with ADHD, I have so many note books and journals" (5★, 30 Sep 2026) — [Trustpilot p2](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=2)
  - "The focus aspect for ADHD (and even PTSD) cannot be overstated." The reviewer advises new users to "take it slowly." (App Store, 19 Nov 2024) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fabulous-daily-habit-tracker/id1203637303?see-all=reviews)
  - Title: "Depression at its finest." The reviewer credits the app with "telling me to slow down and take it easy." (App Store, 1 Nov 2024) — same source
  - Title: "Changed my life!" The reviewer describes depression and addiction, quit vaping, and was 3 months in. (App Store, 9 Aug 2022) — same source
  - Title: "Skeptically positive." Quote: "I was in a pretty dark depression." — [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1203637303/fabulous-daily-routine-planner/reviews)
  - Title: "It grows on you." Quote: "I have ADHD with Autism." The reviewer's mother "gave up after two days." — same source
  - Bustle's author used a "Stop Bed Rotting" ritual during a sluggish winter. (Feb 2026; product-review piece, so editorial bias is possible.) — [Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/wellness/fabulous-app-good-habits-review-features-price)
- **Negative ADHD reviews**
  - A Trustpilot reviewer felt trapped by a subscription and found the app wordy and hard to navigate with ADHD. (1★, 15 Oct 2026; paraphrased by the fetch tool) — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co)
  - "It's NOT for people with ADHD as others have pointed out how noisy and labyrinthine the interface is." This is a Reddit quote reproduced, undated and unlinked, by competitor Headway. — [Headway blog](https://makeheadway.com/blog/fabulous-app-review/)
  - A Trustpilot reviewer said "the cost is a barrier for someone like me who really needs the tools but can't afford them." (4★, 10 Oct 2026; the review mentions ADHD) — [Trustpilot p2](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=2)
  - "I never know where I'm supposed to touch in the app to get to what I'm supposed to be doing." (Trustpilot, quoted by Headway) — [Headway blog](https://makeheadway.com/blog/fabulous-app-review/)
- **Count:** Of the 63 Trustpilot reviews in Sample A, 5 mention ADHD. Four are positive (one of those is cost-limited) and one is negative.

### Inferences
- For ADHD and depression users, the **slow habit cadence and sensory feedback** (sounds, check marks, timers) help. The **information architecture and text volume** hurt. The pacing and the UI clutter pull in opposite directions.
- Billing friction hits these users especially hard. One reviewer had a visual impairment and was twice refused a compassionate refund; others cite cost as a barrier.

### Gaps
- No first-hand r/ADHD, r/depression or r/getdisciplined threads, because Reddit is blocked.
- No reviews using the word "burnout" were found.

## Q4: Do users report that it changed their daily routine long-term?

### Takeaway
Long-term success stories exist but are **rare and self-selected**. A handful of reviewers report 3 months to 5+ years of use and say "some habits stuck." Most positive reviews are written **days to weeks in**, often prompted by the app itself. Some long-term users report fatigue: repetitive journeys, glitches, regretting a lifetime purchase.

### Cited Findings
- **Long-term positive**
  - "I have been using Fabulous for almost 4 years now. While I have had to take a few unwanted breaks, some of the habits have stuck." (Trustpilot, via search snippet, 2026) — [Trustpilot NL](https://nl.trustpilot.com/reviews/69bb602ea6f207a99ae8c961)
  - A reviewer halfway through 7 months reports better consistency and improvements in lifestyle, exercise and mental health. (via search snippet) — [Trustpilot NL](https://nl.trustpilot.com/reviews/69c1c4ecd73a599b0a8814b1)
  - Title: "This is the BEST life-change program/app!" Quote: "I have been using this app since it's beginning stages (2016)." — [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1203637303/fabulous-daily-routine-planner/reviews)
  - "real lasting change in my life" (App Store, 26 Jan 2022) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fabulous-daily-habit-tracker/id1203637303?see-all=reviews)
  - "Simply downloading an app, ANY APP, is not going to magically make you feel better about yourself." This came from a cancer-recovery user describing sustained use. (1 Feb 2022) — same source
  - "I wasn't consistent for a while" ("Love this app!", about 3 months on and off) — [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1203637303/fabulous-daily-routine-planner/reviews)
- **Short-horizon praise dominates**
  - Typical examples: "Only three days in so far," "the past two weeks," "I just started," "I am very new to this." — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fabulous-daily-habit-tracker/id1203637303?see-all=reviews), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co)
  - In Sample A, **0 of 63** reviews describe use beyond a few months.
- **Long-term negative**
  - Repetitive content after a few months. — [Nibble](https://nibble-app.com/blog/fabulous-app-review)
  - A roughly 5-year user hit a glitch around day 34 of a journey and regrets a lifetime subscription. (Aggregator, via search summary) — [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1203637303/fabulous-daily-self-care/reviews)
- **Background:** Fabulous originated from Duke's behavioral economics lab (Dan Ariely's group). — [IU Genetics Lab blog](https://geneticslab.medicine.iu.edu/blogs/md-student-news/wellness-tips-check-out-this-fabulous-app)

### Inferences
- The visible pattern is a strong honeymoon in weeks 1–2 (the water ding, early wins), then a split. A minority become multi-year users who report a few durable habits. Others hit repetition, billing shocks or clutter. Reviews can't show the size of each group.
- "Some of the habits have stuck" is about as strong as the long-term claims get. Even devoted users describe breaks and partial retention, not a wholesale routine change.

### Gaps
- There is no public cohort retention data and no independent study of Fabulous outcomes.
- No months-later blog follow-ups were found. The Bustle and ChoosingTherapy authors don't state how long they used the app.
